# D05 | Service Transition and Customer Onboarding

| | |
|---|---|
| **Organisation** | Global technology reseller, systems integrator and Azure Expert MSP (UK) |
| **My role** | Product Manager, Cloud and Managed Services |
| **Scope** | Six productisation initiatives run in parallel: hybrid cloud, SaaS security, backup and resilience, modern workspace, packaged professional services |
| **Focus** | Transition into support, onboarding, readiness governance |

## Context

New services were being sold faster than operations could support them. Handover from sale to support was slow, customers waited too long to see value, and some launches led to repeated critical escalations.

## What I did

- **Wrote clear service specifications.** Each of the 20+ service categories got specifications, statements of work and handover artefacts setting out responsibilities, support expectations and acceptance criteria.
- **Introduced readiness gates.** Go/no-go reviews before launch covered documentation, support ownership, supplier inputs and outstanding risks.
- **Redesigned onboarding.** Journey mapping and stakeholder workshops showed where customers were waiting on steps that added no value.
- **Kept one view of six initiatives.** Integrated schedules, RAID logs and dependency maps, with dashboards for leadership.
- **Governed suppliers.** Tracked third-party delivery against milestones and agreed corrective actions where gaps appeared.
- **Stopped premature releases** when readiness evidence was not there, even under commercial pressure.

## Results

- **30%** faster service transition
- **11** redundant onboarding steps removed; time-to-value cut from **days to hours**
- Repeated critical post-launch escalations resolved
- OKR and KPI dashboards that sped up executive approvals

## What this shows

- Service transition and operational readiness as a delivery discipline
- Release control under commercial pressure
- Coordinating a global matrix of sales, technical, supplier, legal and operations teams
